    Cabin East

    Wok | Steam | Draught. The pub redrawn on the shop's own drawing: 4.9 m wide and 8.8 m deep inside, one double-height room with no loft. A Pan-Asian menu: cabin-style Chinese, dim sum and bao, and a short Thai, Burmese and Korean run, with the same craft beer, cocktails and look. The kitchen is rebuilt around the Pan-Asian menu, run by one chef and one helper, with hot water, a veg sink, make-up air and a goods hoist. The chimney leaves through the back wall and turns to the right side, because there is an apartment above, and nothing is stored behind the building, because that is parking. The room is mirrored: the washroom and hand-wash sit on the right wall so they drain straight out to the side, and the bar moves to the left wall. Every walkway is laid out to standard clearances, with 0.375 m between the sofa tables and 1.0 m between the tables on the right. Left and right are as you walk in from the road.

    Kitchen · sized to the menu

    Bill of quantities (BOQ) for suppliers: every line numbered with its unit and quantity.

    Checked dish by dish against the Pan-Asian menu: about 23 recipes giving 40 choices, run by one chef and one helper (35–45 covers a service).

    • Wok range ×2Cabin Fever (paneer, chicken, pork), the Honey Trap and Salt & Pepa tosses, Noodle Around, Rice Rice Baby, Man, Churian!, Holy Basil and the lunch bowls. The chef owns the wok and the fryer in service: one burner for proteins and sauces, one for noodles and rice. The range is 0.8 m deep, like real Chinese ranges.
    • Steamer, 2 holesThe four dim sum, steamed from frozen in 8–10 minutes, the three bao (bought in frozen) and the Steamer for two, in stacked bamboo baskets that air-dry on a slatted shelf over the dish line. It tops up its own water.
    • Twin-tank fryerK-Pop Wings, Fry Hard kimchi fries, the first fry for Cabin Fever, Honey Trap, Salt & Pepa and Manchurian, and the darsaan noodles. Two tanks keep veg and non-veg oil apart. Used oil goes into a lidded can on the sink shelf.
    • 2-burner stock rangeThe soups, the coconut-curry bases, the pork-belly braise and boiling noodles. A pot-filler tap on the wall above fills 20 L stock pots in place.
    • Rice cookers ×2Jasmine for the curries and sticky rice for dessert; tomorrow's fried rice is cooked each morning.
    • ColdA dual-temperature reach-in (fridge door and freezer door, about 800 L), an under-counter freezer for the dim sum trays and an under-counter fridge in the pass for the sauce bases: about 650 L of freezer for the twice-weekly dim sum batch, bao, fries, wings and ice cream.
    • WashingThree separate places: a dish sink and dishwasher on the left wall, a veg and herb sink on the right wall (so vegetables never meet dirty dishes), and two hand-wash basins (one at the door, one at the prep corner next to the apron hooks). A 35 L heater gives the dish line hot water; both hand basins have their own small heaters.
    • WaterA buried 1,000 L sump under the garden feeds a pump, filter and softener in a cabinet on the right strip, because Goa's hard water scales steamers, dishwashers and heaters. The pump is on the power backup.
    • AirThe hood pulls 4,000–5,000 m³/h; two low-velocity diffusers put about 80% back in front of it, cooled by a 1.5 TR coil, so the cooks work in a cool pool and the hood still captures. The ceiling is non-combustible cement board.
    • DoorsA 0.8 m double-acting door with a vision panel to the dining room, so a cook and a server can never meet it head-on. The rear exit has a closer, a sweep and a strip curtain; deliveries come in there and reach the storage deck on a goods hoist, never past the hot line.
    • Hand toolsA stone mortar, a mixer-grinder and a kitchen torch on the shelf over the veg bench, and the wok tool rail.
    • Batch daysMonday and Thursday mornings on the dumpling bench: all four dim sum folded and frozen, curry pastes, chilli oil, gochujang sauce and the week's kimchi.
    • Taken outThe chimney soup pot, har gow and xiao long bao, the 0.8 m steamer, the separate pre-rinse table, the wider 1.2 m reach-in and the apron hooks on the kitchen door.
    • RequiredHand-wash basins, insect killer, hood suppression with gas cut-off, a gas solenoid valve and LPG leak detector, a heat detector, floor drain with grease trap, make-up air and the Class K extinguisher.

    Outside · right side and back

    • ChimneyAn apartment sits above, so there is no roof stack. The duct leaves the back wall at 2.35–2.75 m, runs right along the back wall, turns the corner and runs forward along the right side wall. It passes an electrostatic precipitator with a carbon odour filter and the fan, then discharges sideways at about 2.6 m, away from the building.
    • GasThe LPG bank (5 × 19 kg: four on the manifold and one full spare, 95 kg, under the 100 kg limit) sits in a locked, ventilated cage on the right side, more than 2 m from the exhaust outlet. Four cylinders in parallel give roughly 60–75 kW in Goa's warmth, against about 55 kW of simultaneous demand on this hot line (confirm the burner ratings with the vendor; if they ask for more, add a vaporiser). A copper line runs along the wall, through a solenoid valve, and enters the kitchen at the back corner.
    • BehindParking. Nothing is stored there. The rear door stays as the kitchen's fire exit.
    • Right sideOnly a 0.5 m strip along the wall can be used: cars pass beyond it. Everything is slim: a wall-mounted backup cabinet, a water cabinet (pump, filter, softener), a slim bin store and a single-row gas cage, with steel bollards along the strip's edge. AC outdoor units sit high on the wall. Changing gas and emptying bins means a minute on the pathway, so do it when it's quiet.
    • MeterThe electricity meter stays where it is, on the inside of the right wall just past the front pier. A hinged, brass-framed portrait covers it and lifts open for readings, with a small vent gap behind. The main switch and RCCB move into the distribution board, so nothing you would need in an emergency is hidden behind the art. It sits above the wall-side chairs of the front right table: the chair backs are 0.92 m, well below the meter; move a chair to reach it.
    • DrainsThe WC pipe leaves the right wall into a sealed inspection chamber, runs forward along the strip under the cages, falling 1 in 40, to a second chamber at the front corner, and turns in to a 2,000 L biodigester buried under the right half of the beer garden. The hand-wash, bar and kitchen water (the kitchen through the grease trap at the back corner) take a separate pipe that skips the tank, because soap and grease kill its bacteria. The tank's outflow still needs a soak pit or another outlet. Diagrams: biodigester drain plan.
    • Power backupA 7.5 kVA online inverter with about 10 kWh of lithium battery, in a slim, ventilated, lockable wall-mounted cabinet (0.35 m deep) at the front end of the right side. It runs the lights, POS and Wi-Fi, the fridges, freezer and coolers, the ice machine, music, CCTV and, most importantly, the kitchen exhaust, ESP and fresh-air fan, so cooking on gas stays safe in a power cut. Roughly 3–4 hours at a normal evening load. The ACs are not on backup. No diesel generator: with the apartment above and parking behind, its noise and exhaust would be a problem.

    Check before you commit

    • MeasurePillars and beamsThe structure positions come from the site video and the old 10.5 m assumption. Mark them on the 4.9 × 8.8 m drawing.
    • ApprovalLow exhaust dischargeA side discharge at about 2.6 m next to parking needs the ESP and odour filter, and usually the panchayat's and the society's agreement. Get it in writing before fitting the kitchen.
    • ConfirmRight side stripEverything outside on the right fits a 0.5 m strip along the wall, with the car pathway beyond. Confirm with the landlord and society that you can use that strip and fix bollards on its edge, and check the fire officer accepts the gas cage beside a driveway.
    • ApprovalSewage treatmentThe plan puts a 2,000 L biodigester under the beer garden. Confirm that strip belongs to the shop (or get the society's written consent, deed cl. 9) and check CRZ. The tank's outflow still needs somewhere to go: the building's own septic tank or sewer if Tulip Residency has one (society NOC), a soak pit only if it is 18 m from any well, 1.5 m from foundations and the monsoon water table stays 1.5 m below it, otherwise a planted filter or a sealed tank emptied by tanker. Expect the panchayat and Goa SPCB to want a sealed system and a consent to operate.
    • SupplyWaterConfirm the source (society line, panchayat connection or a borewell) and who may fill the sump; the plan assumes a 1,000 L buried sump under the garden and a pump, filter and softener on the right strip. Test the water for hardness before sizing the softener. Without water the kitchen cannot open, so the pump is on the backup.
    • SupplyElectricityThe ACs, dishwasher, glasswasher, ice machine, coolers, flash chiller and lights need a three-phase connection of about 15–20 kW. Check what the shop has now; an upgrade can take weeks.
    • NeighboursNoise for the flats aboveThe slab is their floor. Speakers sit on rubber pads with no bass speaker on the slab, the exhaust fan is on anti-vibration mounts, and music stays low after 10 pm (Goa's loudspeaker rules apply then anyway).
    • NormsAccessibilityThe entry is step-free (a 15 mm bevelled sill). The WC is too small for a wheelchair user (that needs about 1.5 × 1.75 m); if the licence or local rules require an accessible toilet, the washroom block has to be redesigned.
    • LicenceBar licenceConfirm the Goa Excise fee and conditions before building the bar.
    • NormsOne unisex washroomCheck whether 28 covers with alcohol needs separate men's and women's washrooms or an accessible one. There is now one unisex WC and no urinal.
    • StructureStorage deckSize it for about 2.5 kN/m² storage and confirm it is treated as storage, not a floor.
    • ElectricalMeter behind a portraitFine if the cover is hinged or lift-off (not fixed), the meter is never sealed in, and the main isolator sits in the distribution board. Ask the electricity department whether the meter is read remotely (it looks like a prepaid smart meter) and tell them where it is.
    • ElectricalBackup sizingHave the electrician list the backed-up circuits and size the inverter and battery. Wire the gas solenoid so gas cuts off if the exhaust stops.
    • LicenceMusicRecorded music in public usually needs PPL and IPRS (or Novex) licences.
