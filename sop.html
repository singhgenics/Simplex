Non-negotiable

The Cabin Code

Twelve rules. They apply to the owners as much as to anyone else. Break one and you fix it on the spot, then tell the owner.

  • Greet in 30 secondsEye contact, smile, "Welcome to Cabin East." Even if you are carrying plates.
  • Water and menus in 2 minutesEvery table, every time, before anything else.
  • No ticket, no cooking, no pouringNothing is made on a spoken order. Everything goes through the tablet.
  • Ask about allergiesOn every table. Type it into the ticket and say it to the chef.
  • Repeat the order backThe server to the guest. The station calls "Heard" when the ticket prints.
  • Hot is hot, cold is coldClean rim, no chips, no cracks. A dish that fails the pass check is remade, not served.
  • Wash hands for 20 secondsAfter the toilet, bins, phone, raw food, cash and any break.
  • Phones stay in pocketsOn the floor, behind the bar and at the pass. Music and the tablet are the only screens.
  • Never argue with a guestListen, fix it, tell the owner. The guest does not leave unhappy without an owner knowing.
  • Alcohol is 21 and over, and soberNo one under 21. No one who is drunk gets another drink. No exceptions, even for regulars.
  • Clean as you goNothing dirty sits for more than 2 minutes. A spill is wiped in 60 seconds.
  • Thank every guest at the door"Thank you, see you soon." By name if we know it.

Who covers which tables

Floor tables are W1–W4 (window), B1–B4 (bar seats) and G1–G2 (garden), as set up in the POS.

Server 1 · FloorW1–W4 and bar seats B1–B4. Greets the door. Owns the room mood: music, lights, temperature.
Server 2 · Garden and passG1–G2. Owns the pass: every bell, every plate that leaves the kitchen. Owns the bill desk.
Nobody owns a waiting tableIf a guest has waited 30 seconds, the nearest server greets them. If a bell is ringing, whoever is free runs it.
The system

KOT and BOT

The tablet is the only way an order reaches the kitchen or the bar. When the server taps Send, the POS splits the ticket by item. Food prints as a KOT in the kitchen. Drinks print as a BOT at the bar. Both appear on the kitchen screen with a timer. Nobody carries a paper order, and nobody shouts an order across the room.

Who does what

Role SOPs

Each card is the bare minimum for one role. If you are unsure, do the card.

Servers · the two owners

Run the room, the guest experience and the money.

60 minutes before opening

  • Clean uniform, hands washed, phone silent and in your pocket.
  • Tablet charged, logged in with your own PIN, clocked in.
  • Walk every table: cutlery, chopsticks, napkin, condiments, menu clean and unbent.
  • Music, lights and AC set. Garden fans checked.
  • Ask the chef for the 86 list and the special. Taste the special.
  • Two-minute huddle with everyone: bookings, specials, who is on which zone.

During service

  • Greet, seat, water and menus. Say the special and the one dish you recommend.
  • Take drinks and food in the same visit. Repeat the order back.
  • Ask "any allergies?" at every table, then type it into the ticket.
  • Tap Send before you leave the table. Check the printers fired.
  • Serve drinks first, then the full table's food together. Say what each dish is. Never reach across a guest.
  • Check back at first bites, not before and not later. Offer the next drink at a quarter glass.
  • Clear plates as each guest finishes. Wait for the whole table before taking the bill away.
  • Offer the bill when the table slows. Print it within 2 minutes of the ask.
"Welcome to Cabin East. Table for three? Please sit wherever you like, I'll bring water and menus right now."

After closing

  • All open orders closed or voided with a reason. Day close done: cash counted, Z report printed.
  • Tomorrow's bookings and specials noted. Tablets on charge.

Bartender

Draught, spirits, coffee and zero-proof. The bar is also the room's heartbeat.

Before opening

  • Check the 3 kegs, CO₂ and the beer cooler temperature. Pull and discard the first pour. Clean the taps and drip tray.
  • Ice machine full, garnishes cut and covered, mixers and syrups labelled with date, glasses polished and on the rack.
  • BOT printer has paper. Jiggers, bar mats, bell, table tags in place.
  • Stock against the par list. Tell the owner what is low before opening.

During service

  • Make drinks in the order the BOTs print. Say "Heard" out loud.
  • Beer: clean glass, tilted 45°, about 2 cm of head, never topped with a finger. A chipped or marked glass goes to the sink, never to a guest.
  • Every spirit is measured with a jigger. The POS counts stock by the ml, so the pour must match the ticket.
  • Place finished drinks on the bar pass with the table tag, ring the bell once.
  • Stay at the bar. Wipe as you go, glasswasher loaded and run, nothing left dripping on the counter.
  • Alcohol only for guests aged 21 and over. If someone looks under 25, ask for ID. Stop serving anyone who is drunk and call the owner.

After closing

  • Flush the lines and clean the tap nozzles. Cap the kegs. Count spirits against the POS. Log waste.
  • Empty ice, wipe the bar and back bar, run the glasswasher last, mop.

Chef

Owns every plate that leaves the pass, and the kitchen's rhythm.

Before opening

  • Gas on at the solenoid, leak detector green, extinguishers in place, exhaust and fresh-air fans running.
  • Check every fridge and freezer temperature and write it on the log: fridge 5 °C or below, freezer −18 °C or below.
  • Mise en place to the day's par: sauces mixed and labelled with the date, proteins portioned and covered, dim sum counted, rice cooked, garnish cut.
  • Stock check against the order list. Tell the servers what is 86 and what the special is.
  • Taste the soup and the main sauces. If a base is off, remake it now.

During service

  • Read each ticket aloud when it prints. The helper answers "Heard".
  • Fire in order: steam, soup, wok. The whole table lands together within 2 minutes.
  • Oldest ticket first unless it says Serve first. Say the table number when you fire.
  • Allergy ticket: clean board and utensils, helper wipes the wok station, the chef cooks and passes it personally and tells the server by name which table it is for.
  • Pass check on every plate. Chicken and pork reach 75 °C at the centre. Hot dishes go out hot, cold stays cold. Wipe the rim.
  • Tap Ready when plated. Ring the bell once. Nothing waits on the pass for more than 90 seconds. If it does, remake it.
  • At 12 minutes, call the table number to the server.

After closing

  • Label, date and cover everything saved. First in, first out. Discard anything past its date.
  • Gas off at the solenoid. Hood filters out for cleaning. Floors and drains clean. Waste logged and counted into the POS.
Steam ≤ 8 minWok ≤ 12 minTable ≤ 15 minBell once

Kitchen helper

The chef's second pair of hands. Prep, steam, plate, keep the line stocked.

Before opening

  • Hands washed, apron clean, hair covered. Gloves on for ready-to-eat food.
  • Chop, portion and label to the chef's list. Steamers filled and heated. Plates warm, garnish ready.
  • Colour-coded boards in the right place: raw and cooked never share a board or a knife.

During service

  • Answer "Heard" to every ticket. Put dim sum into the steamer the moment the ticket is read.
  • Keep the line stocked: sauces topped up, rice ready, spare plates at the pass. Tell the chef when anything is down to a quarter.
  • Plate to the portion spec, wipe the rim, hand to the chef for the pass check.
  • Wipe your station every 15 minutes. Waste goes straight to the bin, not onto the counter.

After closing

  • Steamers emptied, descaled weekly, and left open to dry. Boards and knives washed and stored.
  • Prep list for tomorrow written for the chef.

Cleaner

Guests judge a place by its toilet and its tables. This role is that promise.

Before opening

  • Floor mopped, tables wiped and dry, chairs squared, garden swept.
  • Toilet cleaned, soap full, paper full, bin empty, floor dry, no smell.
  • Dish station set: sink clean, sanitiser mixed, dishwasher ready.

During service

  • Toilet check every 30 minutes. Write the time and your initials on the sheet on the door.
  • Clear finished plates within 2 minutes and carry them to the dish station.
  • Reset each table within 3 minutes: wipe, set, check, set cutlery, napkin, condiments, clean menu.
  • Spill: put the sign down and wipe it within 60 seconds.
  • Dish station is yours. Plates, glasses and cutlery return clean and dry. Bins emptied when three quarters full, never overflowing.
  • No cleaning chemicals sprayed near food or guests.

After closing

  • Floors scrubbed and mopped, all bins emptied and washed, garden swept, drains cleared.
  • Cloths washed and hung to dry. Chemicals stored away from food.

Table reset · six points

The cleaner's checklist, and the servers' when the cleaner is busy.

  • Wipe the table and the edge, then dry it.
  • Chairs squared and wiped. Floor under the table clear.
  • Set cutlery, chopsticks, napkin for each seat.
  • Condiments tray complete and clean.
  • Menu clean and straight. Table number visible.
  • Look from the guest's chair. If you would not sit there, fix it.

Your decision

A promise nobody else in Goa makes

Put the clock on the table. "Your food reaches you in 15 minutes from the moment we send it. If it doesn't, the first round of drinks is on us." Servers say it when they take the order. The kitchen screen already shows the timer, so a late order is visible to everyone. It only works if the soft-launch tests below show the kitchen hits 15 minutes on a full house. Decide after the tests, not before.

Recovery

When it goes wrong

Mistakes are normal. How we handle them is what the guest remembers. Every fix starts the same way: listen, apologise once, solve, thank.

Order is late (12+ min)

  1. Server checks the pass before the guest asks.
  2. Tell the guest the real time. "Two more minutes, I'm sorry."
  3. Bring water or a small bite if more than 5 minutes.
  4. At 20 minutes the owner visits the table and decides on a gesture.

Wrong or cold dish

  1. Apologise once. Take the dish away at once.
  2. Remake first and sort out the cause later.
  3. Void with the reason on the tablet.
  4. Chef is told the same minute.

Allergy concern

  1. Stop. Do not serve the dish.
  2. Chef remakes with clean tools. Owner checks the plate.
  3. If a guest has a reaction: ask if they have medication, call 108, and tell an owner.

Guest complaint

  1. Listen without interrupting. Do not defend.
  2. Fix what you can inside your authority.
  3. If it needs more, bring the owner. Do not argue with the guest.

Drunk or rowdy guest

  1. Stop serving alcohol. Offer water and food.
  2. An owner speaks to the guest calmly.
  3. If they will not calm down, ask them to leave. Never touch a guest.

Rush: the rail is full

  1. Six or more tickets on the rail means a rush.
  2. Servers quote a longer wait up front: "about 20 minutes".
  3. The helper does only plating. The cleaner takes the dish station only.

Power cut

  1. Backup runs lights, POS, cold storage and exhaust. The ACs are off.
  2. Open the garden doors. Tell guests ten seconds after.
  3. Do not open fridges.

Printer, tablet or payment fails

  1. Switch to the kitchen screen or the numbered pad.
  2. UPI down: card or cash. Card down: UPI or cash.
  3. Key everything in when it is back.

Fire or gas smell

  1. Gas off at the solenoid. Fire blanket or Class K extinguisher for a wok fire.
  2. Move guests out via the main door and the garden.
  3. Call 101. Do not re-enter.

Test the clock

Every number in this playbook is a target, not a measurement. Prove it in practice.

Run three full mock services with friends and familyFill the room: 18 inside, then the garden. Someone with a stopwatch notes the five times in "The table clock": greet, send, drinks, first dish, whole order.
Read the real times from the POSThe kitchen screen timers and the order history show send and ready times. Use them in the first month to see where the minutes go.
Change the number, not the standardIf the kitchen cannot hit 15 minutes on a full house, change the process (fewer wok dishes at once, more pre-batched items) or say a longer wait honestly. Do not pretend.
Still to confirm. The 21 and over drinking age is as printed on the drinks menu. Check it, the licensed closing hour and the late-night amplified music cut-off with the excise department and the panchayat. The FSSAI temperature limits are the common standard, and your food safety supervisor or auditor may ask for different logs.
